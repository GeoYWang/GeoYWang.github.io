QQ Is Not a Contact Option

Why I no longer use QQ, and how to reach me instead

Notice: QQ is no longer provided as a public contact method due to significant security and privacy concerns, documented below.

Summary

A QQ number is one of the most widely leaked identifiers on the Chinese internet, and the client has a history of reaching far beyond what a messaging app needs. This page lists the specific issues that led me to stop using it.

Documented Incidents

Data exposure

QQ numbers as a master key in social-engineering databases

  • QQ numbers are among the most common lookup keys in “social engineering databases” (社工庫) circulating on Telegram.
  • A single QQ number can be linked to the bound phone number, old passwords, real names, addresses and other accounts, which makes doxxing (開盒) trivial.
  • In 2013, a leaked database of QQ group memberships (QQ群關係數據) exposed tens of millions of users and the groups they belonged to — schools, workplaces and families included.
Privacy

Desktop client reading browser history

  • In early 2021, users found that PC QQ and TIM were reading the local history files of installed web browsers.
  • Tencent acknowledged the scanning and said it was used to detect malicious logins, but the access went well beyond what a chat client needs.
User control

The “difficult decision” (艱難的決定)

  • During the 2010 “3Q War” (3Q大戰), QQ told users it would stop working on any computer running 360 security software, forcing people to choose between their messenger and their antivirus.
  • It showed that the client is willing to use its user base as leverage against the users themselves.
Censorship

Content scanning and surveillance

  • Files shared in chats and groups are scanned on the server and can be blocked as “violating” (文件違規) without explanation.
  • Keyword filtering affects group chats and private messages.
  • Posts on QQ Zone (QQ空間) can be removed retroactively.
  • Accounts and groups can be frozen (凍結) or dissolved without a meaningful appeal process.

In addition:

  • Standard chats have no end-to-end encryption; messages and files are stored on Tencent's servers.
  • Real-name phone binding ties every account to a legal identity.
  • Account data is shared across Tencent products, including WeChat.

If You Still Use QQ

  1. Hide your QQ number from search and turn off “find me by phone number”.
  2. Never reuse your QQ password anywhere else, and enable login protection.
  3. Keep the desktop client off computers you use for anything sensitive.
  4. Lock down QQ Zone and remove old posts that reveal personal details.
  5. Leave groups you no longer need; group membership itself is personal data.
  6. Consider migrating to another platform entirely.
